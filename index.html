From zero to hero.

Six phases, numbered 0 through 5 the way the job actually runs. The agent does the work; the pen stays in your hand at every checkpoint.

  1. 0 Brief.Product truth crawled from your repo and docs; one focused interview; direction played back in plain sentences. you confirm the direction
  2. 1 Encode.The brief becomes PRODUCT.md and a DESIGN.md seed: positioning, personality, anti-references, judgment calls marked for veto. you veto the judgment calls
  3. 2 Explore.Three or four divergent hero comps, then a ~6-voice type audition on the winner: live, switchable, screenshot-verified. you pick; grafts welcome
  4. 3 Build.DESIGN.md graduates to locked tokens; the agent builds the full page in the winning voice and exercises every behavior in the live DOM. your copy edits are sacred
  5. 4 Gates.Four audits with fixes applied: accessibility and UX compliance, a scored technical audit, micro-interactions, a copy review. you review the reports
  6. 5 Ship prep.Self-hosted subset fonts, zero external requests, favicon, regenerable og-image, a 404 in the same voice, and a deployment checklist for your host. you ship it

Deployment itself is deliberately out of scope: it’s infra-specific. The skill ends by handing you a checklist for wherever the site lives.

The median is the enemy.

One comp is a guess. site-craft never bets the brand on a single draw: it diverges first and commits late, at your checkpoints, on the record.

4 directions→ 4 color worlds→ 6 voices× 5 lines→ 4 devices→ 1 hero

That’s what this page’s own hero survived. An olive palette, five headlines, and a floating pause mark did not make it. Their funerals are in the git history.

Slop stops here.

Before “done” may be said out loud, the page passes four audits: findings fixed, not filed away.

  • web-design-guidelines accessibility and UX compliance: skip links, focus, live regions, touch targets, the details everyone skips.
  • impeccable audit scored technical audit: contrast ratios computed from the rendered DOM, not asserted from the stylesheet.
  • make-interfaces-feel-better micro-interactions: interruptible transitions, named properties only, hit areas that forgive.
  • writing-guidelines copy review: runs after your edits, because your edits are the copy.

Where a gate’s generic rule fights this design system, the system wins: recorded as overridden, never silently skipped.
